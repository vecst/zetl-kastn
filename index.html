Why holding

Your familiar shortcuts stay within reach. Hold one to capture a copy, write a quick note, or gather saved notes into something you can paste. You can adjust the hold timing and actions in Settings.

Zetl catches what passes through your hands while you work. Kastn gives those notes a place to be organized, edited, linked, and exported when you're ready.

The first time you run it, a short tour measures how long your own taps and holds take, then has you try each hold for real. A ring in the corner fills while you hold, so you can feel exactly where a tap ends and a hold begins.

The shortcuts

These are the default hold actions. Taps keep their ordinary behavior, except that Replay supplies the next saved item when you paste.

HoldWhat happens
Ctrl+CSave what you copied, with a chance to edit it first
Ctrl+XWrite a quick note. Anything selected comes along; press Esc and it goes back
Ctrl+ASelect everything and save it
Ctrl+VOpen Compose to gather saved notes as Formatted text, Plain text, or TSV table rows
Ctrl+BOpen the Board, where your projects live
Ctrl+TStart a new project from a template
Ctrl+JSwitch between your Journal and your last project
Ctrl+RToggle Replay; then tap Ctrl+V to paste saved items one at a time
Ctrl+PToggle pass-through for now: automatically captured copies you immediately paste move to Passed Through
Ctrl+ZUndo Zetl's last action

Add Shift to use the second lane, with its own active project, so work and personal notes can run side by side. You can change what any hold does in Settings.

Where your notes go

Anything you save without picking a project lands in your Journal, sorted by day. Start a project when you're working on something specific, and Zetl files your notes there instead. With auto-capture enabled, ordinary copies are saved into the active project too. The tray icon turns green while auto-capture is active; copies marked private are skipped.

Always running

Zetl

Sits quietly by the clock and does the catching: quick, out of the way, gone as soon as you're done.

When you sit down

Kastn

The workbench for what you collected. Browse it, organize it, edit it, link notes together, and export to Markdown, HTML, or PDF.

Your notes stay on your computer

Zetl has no account and no cloud. Everything you save lives in readable files under %AppData%\Zetl on your own machine.

Copies that a password manager marks as private are never read or saved, even while a project is collecting your copies. When you export a project to share, a clean copy leaves out which apps and windows your notes came from.

The only time Zetl goes online is when you copy a link to an image: it downloads that image so it can save the picture.

Download for Windows

Download, extract, and run. No installer or account needed.

  1. Get the latest Windows release and download Zetl-…-win-x64.zip.
  2. Extract the whole zip into a folder and keep its files together.
  3. Run Zetl.exe. Its icon appears by the clock, and the first-run tour opens. Open Kastn.exe when you want to organize or edit your notes.

Zetl isn't code-signed yet, so Windows may show a SmartScreen warning the first time: choose More info → Run anyway. It runs on 64-bit Windows, and can't reach apps running as administrator unless it's run as administrator too.

Zetl is in release-candidate testing. Expect some rough edges, and please report what you find.
